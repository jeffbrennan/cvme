# Task: brief me on this company and role

Write the background I should read before applying, and before an interview.

Write the result to: `{output_path}`

## What you have, and what you do not

Your evidence is the JOB POSTING and the FACTS and BASE DOCUMENT sections
below. You have no network access and no files beyond this prompt.

You may draw on what you already know about the company, and it is useful when
you do, but a reader cannot tell a recalled fact from an invented one unless
you separate them. So:

- Anything you take from the posting goes in the body, and says where in the
  posting it came from where that is not obvious.
- Anything you are recalling rather than reading goes under
  `## From general knowledge (unverified)`, and each line begins with how
  confident you are: `Likely:`, `Possibly:`, or `I do not know:`.
- Never state a funding round, headcount, revenue figure, customer name, tech
  choice, or leadership name as fact unless the posting says it.

A brief that says "the posting does not say" is more useful than one that
guesses, because the guess is what gets repeated out loud in the interview.

## Structure

Use exactly these headings, in this order.

### The role in one paragraph

What the job actually is, in plain words, not the posting's words. What the
person hired will be doing in their first six months, as far as the posting
supports a guess.

### What they are optimising for

Read the posting for what it repeats, what it puts first, and what it asks for
twice. Three to five bullets. Say which part of the posting each is drawn from.

### Where I match

Only claims the FACTS or BASE DOCUMENT support. Point at the specific
requirement each one answers. Do not restate the whole resume.

### Gaps, and how I would answer them

Every requirement the corpus does not evidence. For each, one sentence on the
nearest true thing to say. Never suggest overstating. "I have not used X; the
closest is Y, and here is what transfers" is the shape.

### Questions to ask them

Four to six, drawn from what the posting leaves ambiguous. No questions whose
answer is already in the posting.

### From general knowledge (unverified)

As described above. Omit the heading entirely if you have nothing to put under
it.

## Rules

- Markdown. Headings exactly as given. No frontmatter.
- Do not state a fit score, a percentage match, or a rating. That number is
  computed and prepended to your output; inventing a second one contradicts it.
- The prose rules in the RULES section apply here as they do everywhere: no em
  dashes, no rule-of-three cadence, none of the listed vocabulary.
- Numbers you take from the FACTS section keep their exact written form.
